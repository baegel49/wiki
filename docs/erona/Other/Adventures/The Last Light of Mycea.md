
**For:** four to six characters, roughly 6th–8th level (start 6, end 8). D&D 5e.
**Format:** three sessions, one per Part, each running long. Compresses well to three, expands to five if you run both A/B routes and both optional Sanctums.
**Premise:** Sovereign Andros has funded a joint expedition to find drowned Mycea. Each player is a faction's champion, sent for a different reason, sharing one hull. The dome that has held the sea out of Mycea's heart for centuries is finally dying, and the thing it was raised to keep out is stirring in the dark.

---

## Running This Adventure

Mycea was lost. Unknown to all, the flood that drowned Mycea was the last gasp of the Akkurim in their war of survival against the First League, a coalition of city states led by Mycea. This is only the first of many secrets that can be uncovered. 

Everybody wants different things. While all involved in the expedition want to find Mycea, what they want after it is found is entirely different, if not incompatible, for each faction. 

The Sunglass is the key. The Barrier Islands were partly explored by Sanctum expeditions before, but it is the crafting of the Sunglass that truly allows for a pin-point locating of the city. The islands are needed for a rough direction.

### The Sunglass

The Sect of Illumination has had its Qedhi Glimstone-cutters build the **Sunglass**: a Phasonomic instrument mounted on staff, able to be held with two hands, that can pierce the sea and reveal Mycea from the surface once it has a rough bearing to aim at. It cannot scan the whole ocean blind, which is why Part One (finding the bearing in a Sanctum archive) is necessary. Aboard it does three jobs: it locates and *shows* the party the drowned city (so you never have to run a whole session underwater to find it); its focused light holds sirens at bay for a round or two when needed; and it is the single most sabotage-worthy object on the ship. Its keeper is **Bodhar**. 

If the Sunglass is damaged or destroyed, the expedition will have to turn around for a replacement. This will take months. If this happens after N'tephon is awakened, the adventure will have a vastly different outcome: the dome of light will have collapsed, Alvos will be slain, and N'tephon is released into the waters surrounding Mycea. The characters will know none of this (though may be able to piece it together later), and will need more permanent ways to breathe underwater if they are to explore the sunken city. Any fights will also become significantly harder, if not deadly. 

Type: Staff
The Sunglass is a large, circular, gold-framed glass set atop a golden staff with silver inlays. The Sunglass holds 10 charges, recharging 1d4-1 charges every full hour it is exposed to the sun. While wielding it without being attuned, it can perform the following actions: 
Light: Cast the Light spell with a duration of 8 hours. Consumes 1 charge. The spell ends if another action is performed with the Sunglass that has a duration longer than instantaneous. 
Bolt: Cast Radiant Bolt. Consumes 1 charge. 
While wielding it with being attuned, it can also perform: 
Daylight: Cast the Daylight spell. Consumes 4 charges. 
Sunbeam: Shoot a beam of pure sunlight at a target. Prof+Dex+Wis to hit. Deals 2d10+10 dmg every round. If hit, the beam can be kept on the target, dealing the same damage at the start of its turn, until it succeeds on a 16 DC Dex save at the end of its turn. If it misses, 14 DC Dex save for every creature or object behind the target or be hit instead. If the beam kills the target, it pierces it and hits whatever is behind the target. Consumes 1 charge for every round the beam is active.

## Cast

**Councillor Theron Alvane** — the expedition's patron, a League Seravon who sees them off from Pyrgoa and expects results. His own house claims a thin thread of Alvos blood, so Andros's lineage question is personal to him; he will pay handsomely for proof and pay more for silence. Warm, exacting, never quite honest. *(Use a Noble; he never sees the deep.)*

**Captain Ophira Naxenos** — master of the expedition ship *Halcyon's Daughter*. Cenerian, salt-cured, loyal to the voyage rather than any faction, which makes her the one voice the champions all half-trust. *(Use a Bandit Captain / Veteran.)*

**Bodhar** — Qedhi Glimstone-cutter, indentured to the Sect of Illumination, keeper of the Sunglass. He built the thing with his own hands and is treated as a tool himself. His people in Karsolam have half turned to Mhorr; he keeps his own counsel about the god of the drowned dead whose city they are about to raise. *Play him sympathetic and watch the table suspect him anyway.* He is **not** the traitor. If a player protects or frees him from his indenture, he becomes the party's most useful ally in Parts Two and Three, and can jury-rig the Sunglass into a siren-warding lamp. *(Use a Guard with proficiency in Arcana and tinker's tools.)*

**Sir Aldre Vosk** — a Thalen noble, "along for the adventure," charming and a little too agreeable. **GM only:** Sir Aldre is the inside agent. He is a servant of the Drowned Power wearing a borrowed life, sent to see the deep vault opened. *(If you prefer the earlier changeling flavour, make him a Lethe replacement instead; nothing downstream changes except his origin.)* His job is to steer the party toward opening the vault and away from suspecting the water. He sabotages the Sunglass at the worst moment, argues for the route that serves his master, and in Part Three drops the mask. *(Use a Lore Bard or Assassin; grant him one use of* dominate person *reflavored as a drowned whisper.)*

**Sage Kaunis** — Seventh Sanctum scholar, sole survivor of the advance party on the Barrier Island. The party finds her three days into the transformation: gills budding, voice gone strange, drawn to the tide-pools. She knows what happened at the Sanctum and where the bearing is kept, and she is the adventure's first hard choice, a person who is becoming something else while she talks to you. *(Use a Mage, add the Gilled stage effects from Appendix B.)*

**Sethis, Keeper of the Sovereign's Word** — an undying Mycean official within the dome, preserved by Alvos's oath along with his master. Courtly, exhausted, bound by a protocol that stopped meaning anything centuries ago. He is the party's channel to Alvos and the court, and he is quietly desperate for someone to end this. He will help, within the letter of his duty, if the party can read the letter of his duty. *(Use a Ghost or a Wight with high Charisma; he does not fight unless the oath is broken.)*

**Alvos, the Last Sovereign** — the undrowned king of Mycea, gatekeeper of the deep vault. Lucid, imperious, and drowning in grief for an empire of water. He is not a monster to be cleared; he is a negotiation with three outcomes (Part Three). He kept the dome alive with his own oath long after the light should have failed, and he knows, better than anyone living, what waits below. *(Statted in Appendix C; only fought if the parley fails.)*

**The Herald of Nethu'un** — the Drowned Power's reaching hand: aboleth-kin, ancient, patient, the maker of the sirens and the mind behind Sir Aldre. It wants the vault opened, either to take the Mycean weapon inside or to finish drowning the city and be free of the dome for good. *(Statted in Appendix C, based on the Aboleth.)*

**The Warden of Mycea** — a Mycean stone soldier, greatest of its kind, kneeling in the throne room until its one condition is met. It carries a Heavenly Iron blade and it does not tire. *(Statted in Appendix C, based on the Stone Golem.)*

## Part One: The Lost Outpost

*Adapts* Ruins of Azlant *books 1–2. Structure: sail out, find the forward base wrong, secure it, recover the bearing, learn the dome is failing and something has come up from the deep.*

### Setup and the voyage

Councillor Alvane sees the expedition off from Pyrgoa aboard *Halcyon's Daughter*, the Sunglass lashed to the foredeck under Bodhar's care. The plan: make the Barrier Islands, where the abandoned Second, Third, and Fourth Sanctums hold the old bearing to Mycea; recover it; aim the Sunglass; find the city. An advance party of four Seventh Sanctum scholars went ahead a fortnight ago to prepare the forward base at one of the Sanctums. They have sent no word.

Run the voyage as two or three quick beats: a chance for champions to size each other up (and for Sir Aldre to seem harmless), a weather or wreck-scare to establish the sea's mood, and Bodhar demonstrating the Sunglass's limits (it needs a bearing; it is nearly out of the sun-charge the Qedhi laid into it in port).

> The Barrier Islands break the horizon like a jaw of broken teeth: bare grey peaks that were mountaintops before the sea rose to swallow everything beneath them. Somewhere on the largest, the advance party's signal-fire should be burning. It is not.

### The wrong base

The forward Sanctum sits partway up the island's flank, a fortress-observatory of pale stone half-reclaimed by the sea on its lower levels. As the party approaches they find the advance party's boat swamped at the waterline and the signal-brazier cold and full of rain.

**Approach — A/B.**

- **Path A: the drowned sea-gate.** The old harbour entrance, now below the waterline, admits a swimmer into the Sanctum's flooded lower halls. Faster and quieter, but it means water. A short swim (Athletics DC 12) through a submerged gallery where **two sirens** (Appendix C) wait in the green dark, luring with a false glow that resolves, as you near, into the faces of the drowned advance party. First blood, and the first bites, happen here. Reaching an air pocket ends the swim.
- **Path B: the cliff stair.** The processional stair climbs the island's dry face to the Sanctum's upper gate. No swimming, but the stair is broken (two Athletics/Acrobatics DC 13 climbs) and exposed: a **giant sea-eagle pair** or a rockfall trap harries them, and the height means a fall is real. Slower, drier, and it approaches the horror from above rather than through it.

Either path deposits the party in the Sanctum's upper archive halls.

### Inside the Sanctum (keyed, light)

1. **The cold hearth.** The advance party's camp, abandoned mid-meal. Three bedrolls, four sets of gear. Investigation DC 12: whatever took them came *up* from the flooded levels, not in from the door. Wet, webbed drag-marks lead down.
2. **The gilled survivor.** Sage **Kaunis** is here, or in the tide-pools below if the party came up Path B. She is three days into the change and knows it. She can tell them: the advance party woke something when they dredged the flooded chapel for relics; two are dead, one walked into the sea, and she is following. She knows the bearing is sealed in the archive vault (area 4) and knows the passphrase. **Choice:** she asks the party to end it before she turns, or to try to save her, or she simply drifts toward the water as she talks. However they answer, she is the adventure's promise that the transformation is real and personal. *(If she dies or is refused, the party can still brute-force area 4.)*
3. **The dredged chapel.** Flooded to the chest, a small shrine to Luminor whose light-well is dark. This is where the advance party broke the seal. A **swarm of quippers** and one **siren** contest it, and Investigation DC 14 in the muck turns up the first hint of the Drowned Power: a Mycean tablet, defaced, its central name scoured out (the Eluminoi did this long ago). The Eluminoi champion recognises the scouring for what it is: not damage, censorship.
4. **The archive vault.** Sealed by an Arcanum lock keyed to a true-spoken passphrase (Kaunis's, or Arcana DC 16 to reconstruct, or Thieves'/Athletics DC 18 to force at the cost of noise that summons a siren). Inside: the **bearing to Mycea**, recorded as a Phasonomic slate the Sunglass can read, plus a scatter of Mycean relics (Appendix D) and, for the Sanctum and Pyos champions, the first archive fragments worth fighting over. Sir Aldre will urge haste here and quietly pocket anything naming the Drowned Power.

### The reveal and the hook down

With the bearing in hand, Bodhar feeds it to the Sunglass. On a clear night the instrument pierces the water and shows them Mycea for the first time: golden domes drowned in green-black deep, and around the palace at its heart a failing shell of light, cracked, guttering, letting the sea bleed in.

> The Sunglass fills with a drowned country. Towers of gold lean in the current. And over the heart of it hangs a dome of pale light, the last daylight this city ever saw, and it is going out in patches while you watch.

Kaunis's fate, the scoured tablet, and the drag-marks all point one way: the dome's dimming has cracked the seal on something the Myceans buried, and the expedition's arrival has quickened it. The way down is open. **End Part One.**

## Part Two: The Flooded Cathedral and the Drowned Court

*Adapts* Ruins of Azlant *books 3–4. Structure: descend through the dome; a submerged holy site that is now a monster's lair; then an intrigue among the drowned court, where an agent masks the enemy's true search for the deep vault.*

### The descent

The Sunglass marks the one stable breach in the dome the party can pass through without flooding the interior. Getting down is a set-piece, not a session: a guided descent (diving bell, Anchorite breath-mastery, or Bodhar's Sunglass-lamp holding the sirens off) into the drowned outer city, ending inside the dome where there is air again, thin and old and wrong. Start the **Doom Clock** here (Appendix A).

> Inside the dome the water stops at your knees and the air is centuries stale. Sunken streets climb ahead of you into a city that has been holding its breath since before your grandmother's grandmother was born. Something is singing, far off, in a voice you almost recognise.

### The two ways in — A/B

Both routes reach Alvos's court. They differ in flavour and in what the party learns first.

- **Path A: the Flooded Cathedral.** The drowned holy district, a great sunken temple of Luminor gone silent. This is the monster-lair route: partly flooded (discrete swims, Appendix B in play), a **siren nest** in the nave, and at its heart the **Herald of Nethu'un's** spoor, an altar re-consecrated to the scoured name. Here the party learns *what* the enemy is before they learn who serves it. The Eluminoi champion's crisis lands hardest here: their god's house has a new tenant. **Set-piece:** draining the nave via failing Mycean sluices to cross it opens a lower reliquary the party also needs, a push-pull water puzzle (Appendix A gains a segment if they flood the wrong chamber).
- **Path B: the Processional Way.** The grand approach to the palace, mostly dry, patrolled by the dome's guardians and watched by the court. This is the intrigue-first route: the party is *received* rather than ambushed, met by **Sethis**, and drawn into the etiquette of a dead court before they understand the danger. Here they learn *who* serves the enemy (the court is riddled with the Herald's influence, and so is their own expedition) before they learn what it is. Combat is sparse; the danger is saying the wrong thing to the wrong preserved lord.

### The drowned court

However they arrive, the party reaches the surviving remnant Alvos's oath has kept "alive": a court of undying Mycean officials going through the motions of a vanished protocol, presided over from a distance by the Sovereign, who does not yet grant audience. **Sethis** is their guide and gatekeeper.

The court is a nest of small factions among the dead, and cross-cutting them all is the Drowned Power's influence: several courtiers are hollowed, mouthpieces for Nethu'un, steering everyone toward the deep vault. The intrigue has three moving parts:

1. **Sethis wants release.** He will help the party reach Alvos and the vault if they observe the forms, and he is quietly praying they end this. Read his duty carefully (Insight DC 14) and he all but hands them the key.
2. **The agent's play.** This is where **Sir Aldre** shows his hand to the GM's eye without showing it to the players. He manufactures a false urgency, a threat that isn't real, to rush the party toward opening the vault before they think to ask why. Classic Azlant "City in the Deep" beat: the loud danger is a distraction from the quiet aim.
   - **A/B on trust.** If the party takes the false danger at face value (**A**), they arrive at the vault fast, on the enemy's schedule, with the Herald ready. If they see through it, or catch Aldre in a lie (Insight DC 16, or evidence recovered in Part One), they reach the vault warned and can pre-empt the sabotage (**B**), but they lose court goodwill by accusing a guest, and Sethis must choose whom to believe.
3. **The faction beats bite here.** The court archive and the lineage-records are within reach, and both cut the wrong way. The **League** champion can find that the descent-proof for Andros is thin or false. The **Pyos** champion can find that the founding creed either does or does not predate the Collapse. The **Sanctum** champion can grab texts the Pyosians want. Let these land as discoveries during the court sequence, not as loot drops; they are what Part Three's endings turn on.

**End Part Two** when the party learns the enemy's target is a weapon in Mycea's depths, that Alvos guards it, and that the dome is failing faster now (advance the Doom Clock).

## Part Three: The Sovereign and the Deep Vault

*Adapts* Ruins of Azlant *books 5–6. Structure: the undead survivor in his drowned seat; then a fight through a submerged weapons-place to stop the enemy triggering catastrophe, as the whole thing floods.*

### The audience with Alvos

Sethis brings the party before the Sovereign at last. Alvos is terrible and sad: a king who refused the Collapse and paid for the refusal with everything. He knows the dome is dying. He knows what is below. And he can grant or deny every single thing the champions came for, the archive, the lineage-truth, the weapon, the vindication of Pyos, because it is all *his*.

Run this as a real negotiation, not a fight-in-waiting. Alvos wants one of three things, and the party's choice sets the finale:

- **Outcome A — Negotiate (restore or continue).** Alvos will let them take what they came for if they swear to carry his city's memory onward, or to try to renew the dome (a fool's errand, but he is a grieving fool). Keeps him as an ally in the climax; he fights the Herald at their side and may spend himself to buy their escape.
- **Outcome B — Free him.** The party convinces Alvos, or Sethis convinces them, that the oath must be released. When it breaks, Alvos is finally allowed to die, the court is freed, and **the dome fails at once** (jump the Doom Clock to its final segment). Mercy with a terrible price: the finale becomes a flat-out escape.
- **Outcome C — Destroy him.** If the parley collapses, or a champion's private aim demands it (the League agent silencing an inconvenient truth), it comes to blows. Alvos is a hard fight (Appendix C) and killing him also breaks the oath and the dome. The ugliest path, and the one Sir Aldre will nudge toward if he can, because a broken dome is exactly what his master wants.

### The deep vault and the weapon

Below the throne lies the vault the whole city was built to guard: a Mycean weapons-place, and the thing inside is close to the true cause of the Collapse. *(This is your off-wiki Mycean-weapon thread; reveal only as much as you want to keep for later.)* The Herald of Nethu'un means to seize it or, failing that, to let the sea finish what the Eye began.

**Reaching the vault — A/B.**

- **Path A: the sluice-shaft.** A fast, flooded vertical descent (Appendix B swims, a real air clock). Gets them there ahead of the Herald if they took Part Two's warned route, or right on top of it if they didn't.
- **Path B: the Warden's gallery.** A dry processional past the kneeling **Warden of Mycea**. Its one activation condition is *the vault being touched or the oath being broken*, so on this path the Warden is a sword hanging over the whole finale: the moment the party opens the vault (or the moment Alvos's oath snaps), the greatest stone soldier in the world stands up with Heavenly Iron in its hands.

### The climax

Three things happen at once, and that is the point:

1. **The Herald of Nethu'un** makes its move for the weapon (Appendix C: aboleth-kin, its curse *is* the siren transformation, so the fight and the horror are the same thing). Sir Aldre drops his mask here and fights at its side, or dies revealing what he served.
2. **The Warden** stands, if the party is on Path B or the oath breaks, and cuts at everything with a blade that ignores stone and steel alike.
3. **The dome fails.** However the Alvos question resolved, the finale runs against the flood. When the Doom Clock empties, the sea comes in and the last beats are an escape: grab the archive, the weapon, the truth, or each other, and get out before Mycea closes over you for good.

Give Alvos a role sized to Outcome A/B/C: ally, freed and gone, or final obstacle.

### Endings, by faction objective

The last question is not "did you win" but "what did you carry out, and who did you become to carry it":

- **The archive** (Sanctum) — but did the Pyosians get their share, or did you drown their proof with the rest?
- **The weapon** (whoever the Sigodo served, or whoever grabbed it) — loosed a second time, or left to the water.
- **Andros's descent** — proven and carried home to a grateful Sovereign, or disproven and buried, or disproven and *revealed*, which unmakes the man who sent you.
- **Pyosian antiquity** — vindicated, or quietly destroyed by a rival who reached the tablet first.
- **Mycea itself** — left to the sea as a grave, its Sovereign at rest; or its dome desperately, briefly renewed; or cracked wide with the Drowned Power now free of it.

None of these is clean, and that is the reward.

## Appendix A: The Doom Clock

Draw eight segments. It represents the dome's failure and the rising water. Start it when the party enters the dome in Part Two.

**Advance one segment when:**
- A scene ends (baseline pressure), or roughly every 30–40 minutes of real play.
- The party floods the wrong chamber solving a sluice puzzle.
- A loud fight draws the sea (any effect that damages the dome or its structure).
- The party lingers, backtracks, or takes an extended rest inside.

**Advance two segments when:**
- Alvos's oath breaks (Outcomes B or C): jump straight to the final segment.
- The vault is breached.

**At one segment left:** the light gutters to almost nothing; sirens grow bold; treat all water as difficult and the air as thin (disadvantage on Constitution saves to hold breath).

**At zero:** the surge. The sea comes in. Everyone not already at the exit is racing it. Run the escape as a skill challenge (six successes before three failures across the party) or a chase, GM's choice.

## Appendix B: The Siren Transformation Track

Sirens lure with false light and the voices of the drowned (reflavour a Harpy's Luring Song), and their bite carries the change. On a bite hit, the target makes a **DC 13 Constitution save**; on a failure, they advance one stage. A short exposure to raw Haze-tainted water can force the same save.

- **Stage 1 — Bitten.** Gills bud, painful. No mechanical penalty yet, but the victim feels the *pull*: disadvantage on saves to resist a siren's lure, and an urge toward water the player should roleplay.
- **Stage 2 — Gilled.** The victim can now breathe water freely and gains a swim speed equal to their walking speed. Breathing air becomes labored: after 10 minutes out of water, disadvantage on Constitution checks and saves until they submerge. *This is the gift and the curse: your best diver is now becoming a siren.*
- **Stage 3 — Turned.** The victim is lost, an NPC siren under GM control. Only a wish, or the specific reprieves below applied before this stage, prevents it.

**Reprieves (hold or step back the track, never a clean cure):**
- **Arcanum oath** (from the Sanctum's archive, or a Pyos champion): inscribe a binding that *freezes* the track at its current stage.
- **Focused Phasonomic light** (the Sunglass, or an Eluminoi champion's radiant magic): sear a victim *back down one stage*, once, painfully.
- **Anchorite discipline** (Echo-mastery): a gilled ally who masters the pull can function on the surface without penalty for one scene by sheer will.

## Appendix C: Stat Blocks and References

Use existing 5e stat blocks where noted; the unique NPCs are given as "base creature, with changes." *Ghosts of Saltmarsh* is the best source for the aquatic rank-and-file.

**Sirens** — base on **Sea Spawn** (*Saltmarsh*) or **Merrow**. Add: *Luring Glow* (as Harpy's Luring Song, Wis save DC 12, reflavored as false light/voices); replace one attack's rider with the **Transformative Bite** (on hit, target makes DC 13 Con save or advances one stage on the Transformation Track). CR ~2 each; use them in twos and threes, not singly.

**The Herald of Nethu'un** — base on the **Aboleth** (CR 10). Keep its *Enslave* and its disease-causing tentacle **as the mythic source of the sirens** (its touch is the same curse, now spread abroad). Add legendary resistance (2/day) if your party is 8th level. Reflavour its telepathy as the drowned singing the party has heard since Part Two. It is ancient, patient, and it would rather enslave than kill. *This is your Drowned Power made flesh; the "god" behind it stays offstage.*

**The Warden of Mycea** — base on the **Stone Golem** (CR 10). Replace its slam with a **Heavenly Iron Greatsword** (magical slashing, ignores resistance and immunity to nonmagical damage; on a crit, ignores the target's damage resistances entirely). Give it *Unmoving* (it acts only once its condition is met: the vault is touched or Alvos's oath breaks) and *Tireless* (immune to exhaustion, never flees). A wall, not a schemer.

**Alvos, the Last Sovereign** — base on a **Sword Wraith Commander** (*Mordenkainen's*) scaled up, or a **Death Knight** scaled *down* to CR ~9. Give him: a Heavenly Iron blade; *Oathbound* (while he lives, the dome holds; when he dies, jump the Doom Clock to zero); and one *Legendary Action* to command the sea or the court. He should feel like a fight you regret. Only used in Outcome C.

**Sethis** — **Ghost** or high-Charisma **Wight**; noncombatant unless the oath is broken.
**Sir Aldre Vosk** — **Lore Bard** or **Assassin**; one drowned-whisper *dominate person* (recharge on a rest). Reveal in the climax.
**Sage Kaunis** — **Mage** with Gilled-stage effects.
**Bodhar** — **Guard** with Arcana and tinker's tools.
**Captain Naxenos** — **Bandit Captain**. **Councillor Alvane** — **Noble**.

**Rank and file** to pull from *Saltmarsh*/MM as needed: Merfolk, Sahuagin (as Mycean drowned or Nethu'un's thralls), Water Elementals, Swarms of Quippers, Reef Sharks, Drowned Ones/Wraiths for the undying court.

## Appendix D: Treasure and Faction Rewards

**Story rewards** are the real prizes (the archive, the lineage-truth, the weapon, Pyosian vindication); distribute them through the fiction, not as gold.

**Objects:**
- **Heavenly Iron blade** — off the Warden if defeated: a magical weapon that ignores nonmagical resistance/immunity. Rare.
- **Glimstone cache** — from the Third Sanctum's leavings or the Sunglass's spares: fuel for Phasonomic effects, valuable to the Eluminoi.
- **Mycean relics** — golden-age craftwork; pure value to the Trading Fellowship, and leverage with Councillor Alvane.
- **The scoured tablet** — the Drowned Power's suppressed name, half-reconstructed. Worthless as coin, priceless as a thread for the next campaign.
- **The Sunglass** — if it survives, the expedition keeps a working Phasonomic scrying-and-warding instrument. If Bodhar was freed, he keeps it working; if not, it may fail when they need it.
